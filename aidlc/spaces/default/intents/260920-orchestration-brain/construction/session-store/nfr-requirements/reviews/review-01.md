### Summary

The author's five flagged risk areas all hold up under verification: the `[N3]` `noeviction` finding is well-grounded (every key in this design, including the co-located `K-11` work-item collection, carries a TTL, and U10 is Redis's first real consumer in this codebase today, so the recommendation is sound); the `[N1]`/`RecentConversations` ownership gap is independently confirmed at zero hits across the three named stages; the capacity arithmetic's division is correct and its "2.8× headroom" framing is reasonable, if slightly under-justified (R-03); `OPEN-N10-3`'s cross-unit gap is correctly sourced from `K-09.data_shape`'s note and `K-11`'s storage line; and the three latency budgets are internally consistent with `NFR3`'s own measurement definition. The two Major findings this review surfaces are genuine, newly-found contradictions the author did not flag as risk areas: (1) an already-approved entity constraint (`EC6`) that, read literally, forbids logging the one field (`sessionKey`) that this same stage's own observability requirements mandate logging, and (2) a "hard ceiling" claim (`NFR4.41`) that the artifact's own capacity math implicitly relies on but that has no actual enforcement mechanism anywhere in the design, since `SessionMessage.text` carries no documented length bound at any layer. Neither is a blocking architectural flaw — both are resolvable by a documented clarification or a follow-up constraint — which is why the verdict is READY despite two Major findings (at the ≤2 threshold).
