### Summary

The repo-fact spine of this artifact is the strongest I have seen in this record — every claim I could check about `langgraph_runtime.py`, `advice_stream_router.py`, `collab_router.py`, `openapi.json`, `ci.yml` and the DAG derivation held, and the `[C9]`/`[C10]` conflict was handled without absorbing the rejected half. The problems are elsewhere.

**Blocking, in my judgement — worth resolving before Construction:** **R-01** (a cross-unit dependency `U11 → U12` that the approved DAG does not carry, mis-labelled as an out-of-DAG boundary; `delivery-planning` reads that DAG and can order the two Bolts wrongly) and **R-02** (two of nineteen spec blocks do not parse, one of them the WS message inventory that two new CI gates are supposed to machine-check — its entire envelope is currently inside a string literal).

**Advisory — cheap text or tracking fixes the human may accept as-is:** **R-03** (name the third OpenRouter entrance and say whether the assertions cover it; the two existing `base_url` constants already differ, so this is a genuine blind spot rather than a documentation nicety), **R-04** (the decision to build a separate layer stands on `[C8]` grounds, but two sentences of its justification are contradicted by the repo and will be inherited as fact), **R-05** (the unenforced facade-uniqueness invariant is honestly disclosed; what is missing is a handoff row so the enforcement question cannot silently evaporate at a CONDITIONAL station), **R-06** (a two-line citation range).

On the question the dispatch asked directly — is an unenforced authorization invariant acceptable to hand to Construction? Yes, provided it is *tracked*. The artifact's candour is correct and the mechanism choice does belong downstream; the defect is that this one item alone got no J-row while every comparable open item did.
